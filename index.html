Scientific Dossier

Why trees matter — and why they are not magic.

This section gives Sylverra a scientific spine: climate context, deforestation scale, biological mechanism and a sober estimate of how many trees would be needed to balance national-scale emissions. The message is strong because it is honest.

The brutal summary

Climate change is already measurable. The IPCC reports that human activities have caused global surface temperature to reach about 1.1°C above 1850–1900 levels in 2011–2020.

Deforestation is massive. FAO estimates about 420 million hectares of forest have been lost through deforestation since 1990.

Trees help in three ways: they absorb CO₂ through photosynthesis, store carbon in biomass and soils, and cool local areas through shade and evapotranspiration.

Important: tree planting does not replace emission reduction. It complements it. Selling trees as a miracle cure is greenwashing dressed as gardening.
Data basis: IPCC AR6, FAO FRA 2020, Global Carbon Project, USDA/Arbor Day estimate, EEA urban cooling research. Values are simplified for website communication and should be refined before formal ESG reporting.

Why more trees means more oxygen and less heat

Through photosynthesis, trees use light energy to convert CO₂ and water into sugars, releasing oxygen as a by-product. The climate benefit is mainly carbon storage and cooling, not because the atmosphere is short of oxygen.

CO₂ + H₂O + sunlight → plant biomass + O₂
More mature tree canopy → more shade + evapotranspiration → lower local surface and air temperatures.

Country-scale tree requirement: reality check

Using a simple mature-tree estimate of 22 kg CO₂/year per tree, the number of trees needed to offset an entire country’s annual territorial fossil CO₂ emissions becomes enormous. This is why Sylverra should communicate contribution, not fantasy.
